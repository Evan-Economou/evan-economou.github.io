Imagine a problem that a computer can solve. It has inputs, some outputs, and something that happens in the middle where the computer solves the problem. We will represent this using the equation $y = mx + b$, where $x$ is our input, $y$ is our output, and $m$ and $b$ represent the rule the computer uses to go from input to output.

In traditional computing, what we have done since Alan Turing took down the Nazis, you would employ an expert in the field to understand the problem fully and write a rule that the computer uses to take your input and turn it into an output. You have $x$, you create $m$ and $b$, and the computer tells you $y$ when you give it all this information. This applies to everything from calculators to internet traffic functioning, all of which rely on manually curated rules and processes.

AI is simply a new approach to computing which I think is best understood by first understanding the motivation for the approach. To that end, take a moment and try to answer the following questions:

1. What is the equation that defines what English word will come next as I write this sentence?
2. What is the equation that defines what a dog looks like?
3. What is the equation that distinguishes a healthy lung X-ray from an infected one?

If you were able to answer one of these, congratulations! Go claim your Nobel Prize and millions of dollars. More likely, though, you are thinking that those equations don't exist, or can't exist. This is the problem AI tries to solve. 

Given a ton of inputs, and a ton of paired outputs, AI systems learn the rule that takes you from one to the other without you needing to understand how it got there. In this case, if you have thousands of values for $x$ and thousands of values for $y$, an AI model will give you the rule to get from input to output, $m$ and $b$. We see this in large language models like ChatGPT or Claude that can answer that first question, or image classification models that can answer the second and third questions. You have also seen this methodology in things as simple as a trend line on a graph, which is one of the distant ancestors of modern AI.

In a sentence, AI allows computers to solve problems and find patterns and relationships that we don't understand.
